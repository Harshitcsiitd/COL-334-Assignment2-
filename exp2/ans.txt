Experiment 2: Report AnswerQuestion: What TCP connection states are traversed during establishment, steady state, and termination?
Answer: The connection passes through SYN_SENT/SYN_RCVD during the three-way handshake and settles in ESTABLISHED on both endpoints. When the client calls close(), its stack sends a FIN and moves from ESTABLISHED → FIN_WAIT_1. The server's stack moves from ESTABLISHED → CLOSE_WAIT and delivers an EOF to the application. As the server and client exchange the remaining FIN and ACK packets, the server transits CLOSE_WAIT → LAST_ACK → CLOSED, and the client transits FIN_WAIT_1 → FIN_WAIT_2 → TIME_WAIT. Because FreeBSD 14 by default suppresses local loopback TIME_WAIT states (verified via net.inet.tcp.nolocaltimewait: 1), the connection initially vanished instantly. After explicitly disabling this optimization, the client side successfully persisted in TIME_WAIT for 2×MSL (≈60 seconds, per net.inet.tcp.msl=30000), proving that TIME_WAIT is owned by the kernel's PCB rather than the application process.  Approach:
Because the teardown on the loopback interface completes in microseconds, a two-layered strategy was used. tcpdump supplied the authoritative packet-level timeline, while high-frequency polling scripts and netstat supplied snapshots of the kernel states that persisted long enough to photograph (ESTABLISHED and TIME_WAIT).
